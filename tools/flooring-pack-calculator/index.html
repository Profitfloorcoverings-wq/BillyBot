How the pack calculator works

LVT, laminate, engineered wood, tiles and carpet tiles are sold in packs or boxes, and you can't buy 0.4 of a box, so the sum always ends by rounding up.

area with waste = floor area (m²) × (1 + waste %)
packs = area with waste ÷ m² per pack, rounded up to a whole pack
m² bought = packs × m² per pack

Worked example: two rooms of laminate

A living room of 4.2 × 3.6 m (15.12 m²) and a hall of 3.1 × 2.4 m (7.44 m²) make 22.56 m². The laminate comes in packs of 1.835 m², and with a bay window and three door frames to cut round you allow 10%.

  1. 22.56 × 1.10 = 24.82 m² needed
  2. 24.82 ÷ 1.835 = 13.52, so 14 packs
  3. 14 × 1.835 = 25.69 m² bought: 0.87 m² beyond your allowance, and 3.13 m² (about 14%) more than the floor itself

At £30 per m², that's 25.69 × £30 = £770.70. Priced per pack, it's 14 × the pack price. Either way you pay for whole packs.

Worked example: herringbone

A 12 m² kitchen in herringbone, allowing 15%, needs 12 × 1.15 = 13.8 m². Say the packs hold 0.8 m²: 13.8 ÷ 0.8 = 17.25, so 18 packs, which is 14.4 m² on the van.

Order it all in one go: batches and shade

Packs are made in batches, and batches can differ. Amtico's Click Smart Parquet installation guidelines tell fitters to "check packaging to ensure all boxes are all of the same product and batch", and warn that "materials from different batches will vary in terms of shade, texture and gloss". Karndean's rigid core guidelines make checking the design, colour and batch the installer's job before anything goes down, and say claims for clearly identifiable defects won't be accepted after installation.

That's why the rounding matters more than it looks. Going back for two more packs next month can mean a different batch that doesn't match. Order the full quantity at once, check the batch numbers when it arrives, and open several packs as you lay: Karndean's guidelines ask for planks to be unboxed and shuffled so you don't get patches of heavy and light shading.

Laying patterns and waste

Waste is the offcuts you can't use anywhere else. Every angle, bay, door frame and pipe adds cuts, and small rooms give offcuts fewer places to go. Herringbone and chevron are cut where every row meets the edge of the room, and a diagonal lay meets every wall at an angle, so both leave more pieces that won't fit elsewhere. Check the pack coverage on the label

Pack coverage is rarely a round number, because it's the planks or tiles in the pack multiplied by their size. Quick-Step's Impressive laminate, for example, has 7 planks of 1380 × 190 mm to a pack, listed at 1.835 m² per pack. Round that up to 2 m² and the 22.56 m² job above looks like 13 packs, but 13 real packs only cover 23.86 m² against the 24.82 m² you need: nearly 1 m² short.

Use the m² per pack printed on the label or data sheet. If you only have the plank size, multiply the planks in a pack by the length and width in metres: 7 × 1.38 × 0.19 = 1.835 m². Check the figure is per pack, not per plank or per pallet.

Let it acclimatise first

Manufacturers set their own rules for letting flooring adjust to the room before it goes down, and they vary by product. Amtico's Click Smart Parquet guidelines say to leave the boxes flat in the room for 24 hours at 18–25°C. Karndean's rigid core guidelines ask for at least 48 hours out of the packaging on commercial installs, and say acclimatisation is generally not essential on residential installs if the product is already at 18–27°C. Follow the instructions for the product you're laying, not a rule you remember from a different one.

Before any of that, the subfloor has to be right. If it needs levelling first, the smoothing compound calculator works out the bags.
