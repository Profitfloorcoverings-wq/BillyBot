How to measure stairs for carpet

Measure the stairs themselves, starting at the top. Guides such as Tapi's measure from the top down because that's the way the pile runs.

  • Steps. Count every step you'll carpet.
  • Width. Measure across each step and use the widest.
  • Tread. From the back of the tread to the front of the nosing. The "going" in Approved Document K is measured nosing to nosing, so a going taken off drawings comes up short.
  • Riser. From the top of one tread to the top of the next.
  • Allowance. Online Carpets' guide adds 5 cm a step for fitting and trimming, and 5 cm either side of the width. Tapi measures each step in one go instead: from the back of the step, over the nose and down to the back of the next tread.
  • Landings. Tapi measures a top landing from the doorway or wall opposite the top step, over its nose and down to the tread, so the landing and top step are one piece.
  • Winders. Approved Document K calls them tapered treads, because the going narrows from one side to the other, so each one is a different shape. Measure each at its widest points.

Carpet per step is the tread plus the riser plus the allowance: 250 mm + 200 mm + 50 mm = 500 mm.

How the strips come off the roll

Stair carpet runs with its length, the way it comes off the roll, up and down the stairs, as the Carpet and Rug Institute's installation standard, CRI 105, recommends. So each strip is the stair width plus trimming, and the strips sit side by side across the roll.

The calculator fits as many strips across the roll as it can, shares the steps between them evenly and cuts the roll at the longest strip. A landing goes wherever it adds least: beside the strips, under the shorter ones or after them. You pay for the full roll width, so the offcut is part of the price.

Pile direction on stairs

CRI 105 notes that most manufacturers recommend the pile runs down the stairs, and Online Carpets says it should run across the landing and down the stairs the same way. Cutting every piece along the roll keeps them all the same way round. The carpet's own fitting instructions come first.

Gripper and underlay on stairs

CRI 105 puts gripper on every tread with the pins towards the riser and, for a waterfall finish, on the risers too with the pins pointing down. Carpet Underlay Shop's guide fixes it at the back of each tread and the base of each riser. The calculator counts both strips per step at the stair width, in the 1.52 m lengths Interfloor sells its stair gripper in: on 0.9 m stairs each length cuts one strip, so 13 steps need 26. A landing needs gripper along its edges too.

For underlay, Carpet Underlay Shop cuts a piece for each tread to sit inside the gripper, and CRI 105 says separate underlay should go over the stair nose.

Landings, patterns and runners

A landing wider than the roll needs a join, and Brintons' installation guide says to keep seams and cross joins away from stair noses.

Strips cut side by side all start at the same point in a pattern, but on the stairs each one has to carry on from the step above, so the calculator adds one pattern repeat per row of pieces for lining up. Check the carpet's fitting instructions.

A runner comes at its finished width, so you need one length over every step, plus any landing it crosses. If it turns a corner, measure each straight run.

Fit stair carpet safely

Loose or worn carpet on stairs is a trip hazard. RoSPA's advice is that stairs should be carefully maintained, and damaged or worn carpet repaired or removed. Stair carpet has to be fitted tight so it can't move or lift, so if in doubt, use a professional fitter.
