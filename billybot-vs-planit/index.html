BillyBot vs PlanIt Flooring: an honest comparison

Two UK products aimed at flooring businesses, built for different jobs. PlanIt Flooring measures, optimises, quotes and runs a shop counter. BillyBot runs the office side, from first enquiry to final invoice. Here is what each does, what PlanIt charges, and which fits you.

The short answer

PlanIt Flooring is a well-made tool that you drive. You map the rooms, run the optimiser, check the margin and send the quote; on its shop plan you also ring the sale through the till. Fast, priced low at the entry end, and clearly written by someone who knows the job.

BillyBot goes at the same week from the other end. It reads the inbox, turns enquiries into jobs, prices quotes on your own supplier prices and labour rates, drafts the reply, prepares the invoice for Xero, Sage or QuickBooks, writes the RAMS and nudges you about quotes gone quiet — all for your approval, never behind your back.

So the honest question is not which is better software, but whether the hours you lose go on building the quote or on everything around it. If it is the first, PlanIt may be all you need. If it is the second, a faster calculator will not fix it.

PlanIt Flooring review: what you get

PlanIt Flooring Limited is registered in England and Wales, company number 17301169. Its story page says the founder spent years delivering technology for large listed businesses, then in February 2025 bought a 50% share in a flooring business with a trade counter, showroom and commercial contracts, and built PlanIt for that shop first. Two products, run alone or together.

PlanIt Estimating

Rooms are mapped by photographing a site sketch, uploading a floor plan or PDF, or speaking the sizes out loud. A 2D nesting optimiser plans every room, stair and landing across 2 m, 3 m, 4 m and 5 m widths for carpet and sheet vinyl, keeps pile direction consistent and lets you move the seams, with waste and margin updating as you build. Stairs, winders, landings and bullnose steps are their own pieces, gripper is automatic, and uplift and prep can be priced per m². Out comes a branded PDF quote, a fitter cutting sheet, a customer accept link and a deposit request, with a CRM pipeline, fitting diary, profit analysis per m², van stock, CIS and reverse charge VAT around it.

PlanIt EPOS

A quick-sale till on one key, barcode scanning, card and cash, stock as rolls, remnants, batches and colours, supplier purchase orders, trade accounts and statements, automatic invoice chasing, cash-up and Z reports, refunds and credit notes, multi-till, a fitter portal, a customer portal and an in-app assistant called Ask PlanIt. Its reports list a Xero-ready invoice export.

What it does not publish

PlanIt says openly that it is flooring-only by design. Its site does not publish inbound email handling, an assistant that drafts replies from your inbox, Sage or QuickBooks integration, RAMS or COSHH documents, payroll or rotas, or App Store and Google Play apps — the mobile app installs from the browser instead. Its pricing page rules out white-labelling at any price, while putting your own logo and terms on every document from the cheapest paid plan up.

Where PlanIt is the better fit

We would rather you chose the right tool than switched for the sake of it. PlanIt is the better answer if:

  • You run a counter. The till, barcode scanning, cash-up and Z reports, refunds and credit notes, trade accounts and multi-till sync are ground BillyBot does not cover.
  • You want the cheapest workable seat. A solo fitter can quote free for 10 quotes a month, or unlimited with their own branding at £19 per user. BillyBot at £149 per seat assumes office work worth handing over.
  • You want to drive every step by hand. PlanIt does what you tell it, in the order you tell it. Some owners want the tool, not the operator.
  • Width shopping matters to you. PlanIt runs the same job against 2 m to 5 m to see which leaves least waste. BillyBot plans on the roll width of the product you specified, so that choice happens earlier.
  • Your inbox is off limits. Most of BillyBot's value comes from Gmail or Outlook being connected. If that is not happening, you are paying for the part you will not use.

Where BillyBot is the better fit

  • Enquiries pile up faster than you answer them. Billy works from the inbox, so a Tuesday enquiry is not a Sunday-night job.
  • Fewer places to type the same thing. One approval moves a quote into Xero, Sage or QuickBooks, and the job, diary and fitters' phones stay in step.
  • Site paperwork holds you up. RAMS with a COSHH section, written for the job in front of you rather than a template with the names swapped.
  • You have fitters. Free, however many, on App Store and Google Play apps, with what they record on site saved to the phone first.
  • You price tenders often. Reading bills of quantities and drawings is on every seat, not behind a top tier.
