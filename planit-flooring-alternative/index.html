What PlanIt Flooring does, from its own website

PlanIt Flooring Limited is registered in England and Wales, company number 17301169. Its story page says the founder spent years delivering technology for large listed businesses, then in February 2025 bought a 50% share in a flooring business with a trade counter, showroom and commercial contracts, and built PlanIt for that shop before opening it to others. It shows: this is written by someone who has stood at a counter.

It is sold as two products that run on their own or together:

  • PlanIt Estimating. Map rooms by photographing a site sketch, uploading a floor plan or PDF, or speaking the sizes out loud. A 2D nesting optimiser plans every room, stair and landing across 2 m, 3 m, 4 m and 5 m roll widths for carpet and sheet vinyl, keeps pile direction consistent and lets you move the seams. Out comes a branded PDF quote, a fitter cutting sheet, and a customer accept link with a deposit request.
  • PlanIt EPOS. A quick-sale till on one key, barcode scanning, card and cash, stock held as rolls, remnants, batches and colours, supplier purchase orders, trade accounts and statements, invoices chased automatically, cash-up and Z reports, multi-till, a fitter portal, a customer portal and an in-app assistant called Ask PlanIt.

Around those sit a CRM pipeline, a fitting diary, profit analysis down to profit per m², van stock for fitters, and CIS deductions and domestic reverse charge VAT on every plan. Commercial adds tender comparison, value engineering, QS-style bill of quantities rate build-ups and take-off from tender packs. The mobile app installs from the browser on iPhone and Android rather than an app store, and works offline.

PlanIt is plain about its edges too: its estimating page says it is flooring-only by design, and its pricing page rules out white-labelling at any price while putting your own logo and terms on every document from the cheapest paid plan up. For measuring, optimising, quoting and running a counter, that is a well-made product with a clear point of view.

Where PlanIt is the better fit

We would rather you picked the right tool than switched for the sake of it. PlanIt is the better answer if:

  • You need a till. If your day starts by opening a shop and ends by cashing up, PlanIt EPOS covers ground BillyBot does not: quick sale on one key, barcode scanning, refunds and credit notes, cash-up and Z reports, trade accounts and statements, and multiple tills in sync.
  • You want the smallest spend that works. A solo fitter can quote free for 10 quotes a month, or unlimited with their own branding at £19 per user. BillyBot is £149 per office seat, priced for a business with office work to hand over.
  • You would rather drive every step yourself. PlanIt does what you tell it, in the order you tell it. BillyBot goes first and shows you the draft. Some owners want the tool, not the operator.
  • You want to compare roll widths on one job. PlanIt's optimiser runs the same job against 2 m, 3 m, 4 m and 5 m to see which leaves least waste. BillyBot plans on the roll width of the product you specified, so width is a choice you make first.
  • You do not want software near your inbox. That is where most of BillyBot's value comes from. If you are not ready to connect Gmail or Outlook, you are paying for the part you are not using.

BillyBot is the better fit if the hours you lose are office hours: enquiries sitting unanswered, quotes waiting to be written up, invoices to key into the accounts, RAMS to produce before a site will let you on, follow-ups nobody gets round to. That is the work Billy takes on, so you win more work and protect your margin without adding a wage.
